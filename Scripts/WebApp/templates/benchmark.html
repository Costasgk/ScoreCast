Model vs Market

{{ n_total }} matches · seasons {{ seasons }} · walk-forward · run {{ generated }}

Prediction sites advertise an accuracy percentage. It is close to meaningless: home teams win about 46% of football matches, so always picking home scores respectably while knowing nothing. What decides whether a forecast is any good is whether its probabilities are calibrated — when it says 70%, does it happen 70% of the time — and whether it is sharp enough to be worth having.

So this page scores ScoreCast the hard way: against the bookmakers' closing price, on the same matches, using log loss and the Brier score. Closing odds are the strongest public forecast that exists — they absorb team news, injuries and the weight of money right up to kick-off. Every prediction below was made fitting only on matches that had already finished.

ScoreCast does not beat the market, and neither does anything else you can buy. The gap is what matters, and it is published here rather than hidden.
